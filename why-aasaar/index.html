Why we’re called AASAAR

In 46:4 the Quran challenges those who worship others besides Allah to back their claim with evidence, even a trace of knowledge. The challenge is in our book, so the next trace should come from us.

AASAAR (aa-saar), from āthār: traces

The Quran issued a challenge

To those who worship others besides Allah, it says:

أَرُونِى مَاذَا خَلَقُوا۟ مِنَ ٱلْأَرْضِ أَمْ لَهُمْ شِرْكٌۭ فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ ۖ ٱئْتُونِى بِكِتَٰبٍۢ مِّن قَبْلِ هَٰذَآ أَوْ أَثَٰرَةٍۢ مِّنْ عِلْمٍ إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ

“Show me what they have created of the earth; or did they have partnership in [creation of] the heavens? Bring me a scripture [revealed] before this or a [remaining] trace of knowledge, if you should be truthful.” (46:4, Saheeh International)

The challenge asks for evidence

Their claim was that others deserve worship beside Allah. The verse tells them to prove it three ways: show what those others created in the earth or the heavens, bring an earlier scripture, or bring a trace of knowledge. A claim without evidence can’t stand.

Classical commentators explain athāra in close ways. Ibn Kathir  ؒ: clear evidence, textual or rational. Mujahid  ؒ: knowledge inherited from earlier people. Al-Hasan al‑Basri  ؒ: something a person extracts and brings out.

So the verse names two sources of proof side by side: scripture and knowledge. We read it further: those who hold knowledge grow in every age, with the physicists and mathematicians of today and whoever comes in a thousand years.

The challenge doesn’t expire

This is the last book, so the challenge stands for every century to come. Knowledge will keep arriving, trace after trace, and every claim it makes is held to the same standard: bring your evidence.

We’re the side that issued it

The challenge is in our book. A book that holds every claim to evidence should be carried by people who can produce evidence, and who know creation, scripture and the knowledge of their age better than anyone.

Instead, others do the research and we show up afterwards with the ayah. If the book said it 1400 years ago, why didn’t we say it first?

AASAAR is where that changes

The name comes from āthār, traces, from the same root as athāratin min ʿilm. Here students start from an ayah, go and find the evidence, and publish it, so that the next trace of knowledge comes from us.
